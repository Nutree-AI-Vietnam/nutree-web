A medium bowl of beef pho (phở bò) usually has about **350–400 kcal**. That estimate is for a bowl of roughly 140 g of rice noodles, 100 g of beef and 350 ml of broth ([VTC News, citing Assoc. Prof. Nguyễn Duy Thịnh](https://www.vtcnews.vn/pho-bo-thom-ngon-bo-duong-nhung-khi-an-nhat-dinh-phai-nho-dieu-nay-ar890071.html), in Vietnamese). A large bowl, extra noodles, fatty cuts, a spoon of broth fat or a side of fried dough sticks all add noticeably more.

This article breaks down pho calories by ingredient, shows how much a small and a large bowl differ, and explains how to order pho when you're trying to lose weight. One general note: every number below is an estimate, and the real value depends on each restaurant's recipe and portion size.

## How many calories are in a bowl of beef pho?

There is no single number for every bowl, because each shop uses different amounts of noodles, cuts of beef and broth. Two common estimates land close to each other:

| Estimate | Serving | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By ingredient, Vietnamese bowl | 140 g rice noodles, 100 g beef, 350 ml broth | about 350–400 kcal |
| USDA FoodData Central, “Soup, pho, with meat” | 2 cups (490 g) | about 377 kcal |

Sources: [VTC News](https://www.vtcnews.vn/pho-bo-thom-ngon-bo-duong-nhung-khi-an-nhat-dinh-phai-nho-dieu-nay-ar890071.html) and [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2707124/nutrients) (77 kcal per 100 g).

So if you don't know a shop's recipe, **about 350–400 kcal for a medium bowl of beef pho** is a sensible starting point. Add to it when the bowl is bigger or comes with extras.

## Where do the calories in pho come from?

A bowl of pho has three main parts: the rice noodles (bánh phở), the beef and the broth. Each one brings a different macronutrient: the noodles are mostly carbohydrate, the beef is mostly protein, and the broth carries most of the fat simmered out of the bones and meat.

![A bowl of plain rice noodles, a bowl of clear pho broth with spring onion, and a plate of thinly sliced beef](/blog/pho-bo-bao-nhieu-calo/thanh-phan-to-pho-banh-pho-thit-bo-nuoc-dung.jpg "The three main parts of a bowl of beef pho: rice noodles, beef and broth.")

According to the Vietnamese Food Composition Table from Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition, pho noodles provide about 143 kcal and lean beef about 118 kcal per 100 g ([Vietnamese Food Composition Table, as cited by Tâm Anh General Hospital](https://tamanhhospital.vn/bang-calo-thuc-pham/), in Vietnamese). For the serving above:

| Ingredient | Serving | Estimated calories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rice noodles | 140 g | 140 × 1.43 ≈ 200 kcal |
| Lean beef | 100 g | about 118 kcal |
| Noodles + beef | | about 318 kcal |

The rest, a few dozen kcal to reach 350–400 kcal, comes from the broth, the fat and the onions. In other words, in an ordinary bowl of beef pho **the noodles are the biggest source of calories**, followed by the meat.

### Macros in a bowl of beef pho

USDA data for “Soup, pho, with meat” gives about 5.8 g of protein, 5.6 g of carbohydrate and 3.3 g of fat per 100 g ([USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2707124/nutrients)). For a 490 g bowl:

| Macro | Per 100 g | Per 490 g bowl |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protein | 5.8 g | about 28 g |
| Carbohydrate | 5.6 g | about 27 g |
| Fat | 3.3 g | about 16 g |

The USDA recipe may use fewer noodles than a bowl served in Vietnam, so pho from a Vietnamese shop is usually higher in carbohydrate than this table. Even so, the table shows something useful: beef pho gives you a fair amount of protein for one meal.

## Small, medium and large bowls: how many calories apart?

Portion size decides most of the total. The table below is an **illustrative example** that counts only the noodles and lean beef, using the Vietnamese Food Composition Table values (143 kcal and 118 kcal per 100 g), without the broth:

| Bowl size (example) | Rice noodles | Lean beef | Calories from noodles and beef |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 100 g | 70 g | about 225 kcal |
| Medium | 140 g | 100 g | about 318 kcal |
| Large | 200 g | 150 g | about 463 kcal |

Going from a medium to a large bowl adds about 145 kcal from the noodles and beef alone. If you often order the “special” bowl (tô đặc biệt) or ask for extra noodles, count that difference in your daily total.

## Rare beef, brisket and the extras you add

Calories change with the cut of meat and whatever you add:

- **Thinly sliced lean beef** (the usual cut for rare beef pho, phở tái) is low in fat, so most of its energy comes from protein.
- **Fattier cuts** such as fatty brisket (gầu), or flank with its fat left on (nạm), carry more fat than lean beef.
- **Broth fat and bone marrow** (nước béo, tuỷ) are almost pure fat. Each gram of fat gives about 9 kcal, twice as much as protein or carbohydrate ([FAO, 2003](https://www.fao.org/4/y5022e/y5022e00.htm)), so just 10 g of added fat is about 90 kcal.
- **Fried dough sticks** (quẩy) are deep-fried dough, so they add both carbohydrate and fat.
- **Hoisin and chilli sauce** contain sugar and salt. A little for dipping hardly counts, but a lot stirred into the bowl does.

<Callout type="note" title="Pho broth and salt">
Pho broth is usually well seasoned. WHO recommends that adults eat less than 5 g of salt a day ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/salt-reduction)), counting the salt in fish sauce, seasoning powder and dipping sauces. If you're cutting down on salt, especially with high blood pressure or kidney disease, you don't need to drink all the broth, and it's worth asking your doctor what amount of salt suits you.
</Callout>

## Is pho fattening?

No single dish makes you gain weight on its own. According to WHO, the basic cause of overweight and obesity is an imbalance between the energy you take in and the energy you use ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight)). A medium bowl of beef pho at about 350–400 kcal can easily fit into a weight-loss plan, as long as your total calories for the day stay below what your body uses.

Beef pho has a few points in its favour too: protein from the beef, plenty of herbs and bean sprouts on the side, and a broth that makes the meal large in volume. The things to watch are big bowls, extra noodles, added broth fat and fried dough sticks, and the salt in the broth.

## How to eat pho when you're losing weight

You don't have to give up pho. A few small changes when you order cut calories while keeping it satisfying:

1. **Order a medium or small bowl**, or ask for fewer noodles (ít bánh). The noodles are the biggest source of calories in the bowl.
2. **Choose lean beef**: rare beef (tái) or lean flank, instead of fatty brisket or extra broth fat.
3. **Eat plenty of herbs and bean sprouts** so the meal keeps you full longer for very few extra calories.
4. **Skip the fried dough sticks**, or have just a little.
5. **Dip into hoisin and chilli sauce** instead of pouring them into the bowl.
6. **Leave some of the broth**, especially if you're watching your salt.
7. **Ask for extra beef instead of extra noodles** if you need more protein, for example when you're training.

<Callout type="tip" title="Ordering pho on a diet">
“A medium bowl, rare beef and flank, fewer noodles, no broth fat” with a full plate of herbs is a simple order that keeps pho filling and lighter in calories.
</Callout>

## Tracking calories when you eat pho out

The hardest part of eating pho at a restaurant is not knowing how many noodles and how much beef they use. A few habits help you estimate more closely:

- **Remember the usual bowl size** at the shop you go to most, and use the same estimate each time so you can compare days.
- **Log the extras separately**, such as fried dough sticks, egg or broth fat, because they change the total the most.
- **Use a calorie-tracking app**: Nutree estimates calories and macros from a photo of your meal, a barcode or a dish name you type, such as “phở bò tái”, then adjusts your targets as you progress.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many calories are in a bowl of pho?

A medium bowl of beef pho, with about 140 g of rice noodles, 100 g of beef and 350 ml of broth, has about 350–400 kcal ([VTC News](https://www.vtcnews.vn/pho-bo-thom-ngon-bo-duong-nhung-khi-an-nhat-dinh-phai-nho-dieu-nay-ar890071.html)). A large bowl, fatty cuts, added broth fat or fried dough sticks push it higher.

### How much protein is in a bowl of beef pho?

According to USDA, a bowl of pho with meat of about 490 g has about 28 g of protein ([USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/2707124/nutrients)). The real amount depends mostly on how much beef the shop puts in the bowl.

### Is pho for dinner fattening?

When you eat matters less than your total energy for the day. If an evening bowl of pho still fits your calorie target, it won't make you gain more weight than eating it in the morning ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight)).

### Can I eat pho every day?

Beef pho can be a balanced meal, but eating the same dish every day makes your diet less varied, and the broth is usually salty. Rotate it with other dishes, eat plenty of vegetables with it, and keep an eye on your salt across the day.
